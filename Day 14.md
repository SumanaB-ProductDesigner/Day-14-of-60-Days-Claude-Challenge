# Day-14-of-60-Days-Claude-Challenge
AI Job red flag detector
Job hunting today means wading through job descriptions engineered to sound exciting while saying almost nothing concrete. That's exactly the gap an AI red-flag scan for JDs fills.

𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲'𝘀 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗳𝘂𝗹: instead of reading a posting once and going with gut feel, it forces a structured pass across five dimensions — unrealistic requirements, toxic culture signals ("fast-paced," "wear many hats," "minimal oversight"), remote work authenticity, hiring practice red flags like missing salary ranges, and company stability. Patterns that are easy to miss in isolation (a vague responsibility here, a hustle-culture phrase there) become obvious when scored and tabulated together.

𝗝𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗮𝘀 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗮𝗻𝘁: it doesn't just flag problems. A good analysis also surfaces genuine positives — funding stability, specific role scope, realistic tooling asks — so you're not left with a one-sided negative read. And it ends with concrete interview questions tied to each concern, turning vague unease into things you can actually ask a recruiter.

This kind of analysis works from what's actually given — the job description text and any company info provided (funding, investors, public reputation). It doesn't have private access to Glassdoor reviews, employee sentiment, or internal company data. It's pattern-matching against known risk signals (buzzwords, structural gaps, tonal shifts), not a verified background check. So it's best used as a first filter to sharpen your judgment and prep smarter interview questions, not as a replacement for your own research, network conversations, or Glassdoor digging.

Used that way, it's a genuinely efficient pre-application screen that saves time and helps you walk into interviews asking better questions.
